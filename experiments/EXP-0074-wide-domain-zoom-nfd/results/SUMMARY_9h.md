# EXP-0074 wide-domain (Sean) validation -- summary at the 9 h mark (2026-10-07 ~07:50); pilot-grade caveats below
Training/validation corpus: Sean (canonical wide corpus), pinned FILE-level split (results/split_files.json), 8 shards (scattered/inbetween/piled x n20/n50/n100, piled_n100 excluded), test = 8 x (1000 rows, <=400 4-push chains, 60 same-state pools). All numbers CLEAN (an earlier split leak was found and fixed at ~04:00; every pre-fix trained-model result was discarded, see LOG).
Headline (mean over 8 test shards; pasted 64x64 world frame; roll_k = rollout accuracy after k pushes, own output fed back): results/headline_table.md
Findings
1. Normal NFD / LF baselines on the wide data: plain 64x64 NFD acc1 .530 (4-push .383); wide-fit switched LinearForesight .539 (.410). Narrow-trained models transfer badly (zoom64 .297, LF .176).
2. Best multi-step model: zoom128 [8,16,32] trained through 4 unrolled pushes: acc1 .582, roll1-4 .575 .515 .479 .464 (seed-to-seed spread <= .002). 4-push accuracy = 88 % of the normal NFD's one-step accuracy and 86 % of LF's; 3-push .479 = 90 %.
3. Most of the gain over the normal NFD is RESOLUTION and CAPACITY, not the crop: vanilla 128 with the same [8,16,32] UNet, also unrolled, gets .572 / roll4 .453. Zoom adds +.010 acc1 and +.010-.012 roll4 (consistent over 2 seeds), mostly on scattered shards (+.012-.022 acc1) and short pushes (L<30 mm +.032), ~0 on piled scenes and L>=50 mm (the window grows with push length: 114 mm at 128 px = 0.9 mm/px vs 1 mm/px vanilla).
4. Zoom has LOWER slateN than vanilla (.899 vs .924; scattered_n20 .798 vs .852): its pasted prediction gains spurious mass that grows with push length. A post-hoc zero-sum-delta fix raises both models' acc1 (~+.02) and slateN (+.04) (chosen on test pools -> being re-done on val pools); a training-time mass term (64 px fine-tune) helps only a little.
5. Unrolled (T=4) training helps rollouts for all models (+.016 .. +.033 at k=4), costs ~0-.006 one-step accuracy for the zoom models (zoom64: -.013), helps vanilla.
6. Capacity matters on the wide domain (unlike the narrow pilot): [8,16,32] beats [4,8,16] by +.007 (zoom128) / +.018 (vanilla128) one-step; [16,32,64] single-step overfits rollouts (pre-fix run, indicative). 2x data (168k rows) did not help at 64 px.
7. Fallbacks: windows constant within 3 length bins (single model) and per-bin / per-object-count specialists showed NO gain (those probes ran before the leak fix: indicative only; zoom128 never collapsed to the normal NFD, so the per-bin split was not needed).
8. Cost (idle GPU, K=1024 candidates from one state, visual input): vanilla128 284 ms, zoom128 361 ms (window out) / 398 ms (pasted to the world grid) = 1.27-1.37x; zoom64 [8,16,32] 88/124 ms vs vanilla64 [4,8,16] 37/42 ms (4x fewer parameters).

Caveats: single narrow-style training recipe per model, 2 seeds (3rd running), the test set is one fixed draw (no row bootstrap), slateN is saturated (~.90-.92) and pools have K~8, LF used its own train/val files (disjoint from test), rollouts are limited to 4 pushes by the chain length of the corpus, accuracy numbers are in the pasted 64x64 frame whose ceiling is set by raster style.
